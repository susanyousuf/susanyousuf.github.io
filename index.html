Causal Inference · Machine Learning · Survey Methodology · Policy Analysis

Much of my work has happened where health care meets everyday life: coordinating fertility treatment, leading on-site operations for community COVID-19 testing, and supporting patients through rehabilitation. Those settings taught me that health is rarely decided in the exam room alone. It is shaped by policy, by access, and by the circumstances people bring with them.

At George Mason University, where I earned a B.S. in Community Health, I began studying those forces more formally as a research assistant on projects examining maternal healthcare experiences and barriers to care. I came away convinced that the questions that matter most in public health are also among the hardest to answer well.

I'm now pursuing an M.S. in Data Science at American University, concentrating in Applied Public Affairs, where I focus on program evaluation and the economics of policy. My training centers on causal inference, survey methodology, and machine learning, toward a simple aim: evidence that communities and decision-makers can trust. As a Graduate Teaching Assistant for Biostatistics and Statistical Machine Learning, I also help other students learn these methods.

The questions that drew me here haven't changed. I'm learning how to answer them well.
